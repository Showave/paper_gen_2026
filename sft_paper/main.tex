\begin{abstract}
Instruction-tuning mixtures contain examples with different sequence lengths,
hardware costs, and effects on downstream capabilities, yet data selection and
physical sequence packing are normally optimized separately. We study
packing-aware, reference-constrained online scheduling: each optimizer step
searches for predicted target-reference improvement while limiting regression
on protected reference losses under an end-to-end compute budget. Our method
uses low-dimensional, optimizer-state-conditioned gradient proxies to plan an
isolated pack, then evaluates the \emph{actual} candidate AdamW displacement on
fresh gate examples before committing the update. A standard smoothness
argument gives conditional one-step loss control only on a joint event of
valid score, norm, and local-curvature bounds; the empirical gate does not by
itself establish that event or imply general capability safety. This
methods-first draft specifies the estimator, algorithm, falsification tests,
and matched-budget protocol, while deliberately withholding empirical claims
until the specified experiments are executed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) converts a pretrained language model into an
instruction-following system, but the available supervision is rarely
homogeneous. A single training pool may mix code, mathematics, dialogue,
tool-use, and safety data; examples within each source differ by orders of
magnitude in length and may induce conflicting updates. Training on every
example is expensive, while selecting examples only by semantic similarity or
loss can improve one target and silently regress others.

Recent selectors estimate target influence from optimizer-aware gradients
\cite{xia2024less}, training trajectories \cite{yang2024smalltolarge}, Shapley
approximations \cite{he2024shed}, or Fisher information
\cite{deb2025fishersft}. Token-level cleaning further shows that useful and
informative and potentially harmful supervision can coexist within one response
\cite{pang2025tokencleaning}. These methods largely treat the training cost of
an example as additive. In practice, the cost of a batch depends on sequence
length, padding, packing, kernels, and the accumulation schedule. Selecting a
fixed set and packing it afterward should change execution cost but, under
correct isolation, not its gradient. Under a compute budget, however, that
cost changes which windows are feasible and thereby couples selection to
packing through the feasible set.

A second difficulty is multi-capability control. Online weighting and mixture
methods adapt data use as the model changes
\cite{yu2025dynamicbilevel,tan2026fastmix,ming2026ideal,zhao2026adapt}, but a
weighted average objective can hide a severe regression on one protected
slice. Conversely, a hard constraint based only on a planning proxy may be
misleading: an additive frozen-preconditioner approximation cannot reconstruct
the candidate-dependent second moment of AdamW.

We ask the following falsifiable question:
\emph{after charging all selection, packing, verification, and rejected-update
costs, does joint pack-level scheduling improve the target-loss versus
worst-protected-loss frontier over selecting first and packing second?}
The question concerns specified reference distributions, not an undefined
notion of general model ``safety.''

This paper makes four scoped contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate SFT scheduling over a complete optimizer accumulation
  window, with a profiled non-additive pack cost and explicit constraints for
  multiple protected reference losses.
  \item We derive an optimizer-state-conditioned planning score while making
  clear why per-example sketches cannot exactly predict an AdamW update.
  \item We apply an independent post-selection gate that evaluates the
  actual candidate displacement before optimizer state is committed, and give
  a conditional one-step loss bound.
  \item We specify a matched end-to-end compute protocol designed to distinguish
  a genuine selection--packing interaction from selector overhead, proxy error,
  and ordinary best-fit packing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Offline selection and valuation.}
LESS stores low-rank, Adam-aware gradient features and retrieves examples
aligned with target examples \cite{xia2024less}. SmallToLarge summarizes proxy
model loss trajectories \cite{yang2024smalltolarge}; SHED moves Shapley
valuation to cluster representatives \cite{he2024shed}; FisherSFT selects for
last-layer-linearized information gain \cite{deb2025fishersft}; and influence
distillation amortizes expensive influence computations through landmarks
\cite{nikdan2025influence}. Large-pool studies caution that expensive selectors
can underperform simple representation-similarity selection
\cite{ivison2025largescale}.
SPICE is especially close: it combines Fisher information with a penalty for
gradient conflict \cite{chang2026spice}. Our target-gradient proxy is not
presented as new. The distinction under study is joint physical-pack
construction, protected constraints, and an actual-update gate.
GIST shows that diagonal optimizer geometry can be misleading for LoRA and
instead scores examples in a coupled target-gradient subspace
\cite{min2026gist}; UDS provides a low-overhead online utility--diversity
selector without reference gradients \cite{zou2026uds}. Both are required
comparators, and GIST scoring is also crossed with our pack-cost planner.
P2D jointly selects task-sensitive attention heads and examples while charging
selection and training latency \cite{chen2026p2d}. Because changing the
trainable subspace changes both gradient geometry and cost, we treat P2D as an
end-to-end comparator rather than attributing its gains to data selection
alone.
PartitionSel couples validation-gradient matching across domains under
partition-matroid coverage constraints \cite{agrawal2026partitionsel}. Its
source-constrained selector is a close matched-cost baseline; our separate
question is whether profiling physical pack cost changes the feasible choice.

\paragraph{Online allocation.}
Dynamic bilevel weighting \cite{yu2025dynamicbilevel}, source-mixture
optimization \cite{tan2026fastmix}, capability equilibrium adaptation
\cite{ming2026ideal}, online per-example reweighting \cite{zhao2026adapt}, and
bandit-based mixture scheduling \cite{shin2026dynamixsft} all recognize that
data value changes during training. Layer-wise studies find family-specific
gradient patterns even when relative quality trends persist
\cite{li2026gradients}. Unlike source-level mixture adaptation, our decision
variable is a complete physical pack plan for one optimizer step.

\paragraph{Compute-aware selection.}
Compute-constrained data selection charges selection and fine-tuning jointly
\cite{yin2025compute}. We adopt that accounting principle and extend the
decision to a profiled pack cost. Merely adding this cost to an existing
selector is not sufficient evidence of novelty: the central experiment must
show an interaction between which examples are selected and how they are
packed.
Packing systems already optimize execution after examples are chosen.
Hierarchical Balance Packing profiles multiple length and parallelism groups
\cite{yao2025hbp}, while SlimPack balances variable-length forward and backward
work with fine-grained slices \cite{liu2025slimpack}. They are systems
comparators in our study. Neither supplies evidence that a data selector should
change its subset because of profiled physical cost.
Threshold Filtering Packing explicitly selects related and diverse examples
for a common pack \cite{dong2025tfp}, and packing analyses compare related
versus unrelated co-packing across model and dataset scales
\cite{wang2025packing}. Those methods permit or study cross-example context.
Our boundary-masked setting instead holds the gradient for a fixed subset
invariant and couples selection to packing only through measured feasible cost.

\section{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

Let $\theta_t\in\mathbb{R}^p$ be the trainable parameters at step $t$; $p$ may
denote an adapter subspace rather than all model parameters. Candidate
instruction $i=(x_i,y_i)$ has packed length $n_i$ and supervised response-token
indices $\mathcal R_i$. Define its unnormalized supervised loss
$\ell_i(\theta)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_i}
-\log p_\theta(y_{ir}\mid x_i,y_{i,<r})$ and
$N_B=\sum_{i\in B}|\mathcal R_i|$. For an accumulation window $B$, the
implementation uses the response-token-normalized loss
\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{tr}}_B(\theta)=
\frac{1}{N_B}\sum_{i\in B}\ell_i(\theta).
\label{eq:train-loss}
\end{equation}
The global denominator is fixed across microbatches so that, with dropout
disabled, isolated examples have additive gradients.

Reference distributions are represented by disjoint datasets
$V_0,V_1,\ldots,V_C$. $V_0$ measures the target behavior; $V_{1:C}$ are
protected slices. Their differentiable losses are $L_c(\theta)$.
$V^{\mathrm{score}}$ may be queried repeatedly by the scheduler,
$V^{\mathrm{gate}}$ supplies fresh post-selection checks, and
$V^{\mathrm{audit}}$ remains sealed until every hyperparameter and stopping
rule is frozen. Source- and template-level deduplication is required across
these resources and the final benchmarks.

A packing assignment $\pi$ maps examples to physical packs and obeys
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i:\pi(i)=j} n_i\le L_{\max}\quad\text{for every pack }j.
\label{eq:packing}
\end{equation}
Every method uses the same fixed capacity of $P$ physical packs, $P$
microbatches, and world size per optimizer update, and must satisfy
$N_{\min}\le N_B\le N_{\max}$. Thus the scheduler cannot obtain a different
Adam step count or accumulation window merely by selecting more packs.
Boundary-masked attention and reset position identifiers prevent cross-example
information flow. Let
$\overline C_t^{\mathrm{all}}(B,\pi)$ denote a candidate reserve intended to
upper-bound allocated
accelerator-seconds for packing, reference gradients, candidate scoring,
solving, gate forwards, training, and rejected updates, with sunk round costs
assigned to every candidate. CPU-seconds and wall time are separate Pareto
endpoints, not silently converted into accelerator cost. The goal is not to
minimize example count, but to maximize target improvement subject to
protected-loss and accelerator-cost constraints.

Under exact isolation, packing cannot change $G_B$ for a fixed $B$. Define
\begin{equation}
\overline C_t^{\mathrm{all},\star}(B)=
\min_{\pi\ \mathrm{feasible}}\overline C_t^{\mathrm{all}}(B,\pi).
\label{eq:profiled-cost}
\end{equation}
An exact joint optimizer is therefore equivalent to selection under this
non-additive profiled cost. Our joint beam search approximates that profile; it
does not claim that packing order changes the gradient. The decisive ablation
separates cost profiling from pack-solver quality.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

At each refresh round we sample a candidate pool, construct feasible pack plans
with a beam-search heuristic, score those plans with a frozen optimizer
surrogate, and select the best plan found within a fixed search budget. We then compute the selected
window's exact gradient, construct the exact AdamW displacement without
committing optimizer state, and apply an independent gate. Rejected work is
counted against the same budget.

\subsection{Training Objective}

For any candidate window $B$, let
\begin{equation}
\Delta_t(B)=\theta_{t+1}(B)-\theta_t
\end{equation}
denote the complete optimizer displacement, including momentum,
preconditioning, clipping, weight decay, and learning-rate scaling. The
first-order predicted improvement on reference $c$ is
\begin{equation}
S_{c,t}(B)=-\nabla L_c(\theta_t)^\top\Delta_t(B).
\label{eq:exact-score}
\end{equation}
Thus $S_c>0$ predicts a loss decrease. For SGD,
$\Delta=-\eta\nabla L_B^{\mathrm{tr}}$ and positive gradient alignment is
beneficial.

Writing $G_B=N_B^{-1}\sum_{i\in B}\nabla\ell_i$, the exact pilot AdamW
displacement is not additive in per-example gradients because its candidate
second moment contains the globally clipped
${\overline G_B}^{\odot 2}$ defined below. For planning only, we freeze the
previous denominator and use the affine surrogate
\begin{equation}
\widetilde\Delta_t(B)=d_t-\eta_t A_tG_B,
\label{eq:surrogate}
\end{equation}
where diagonal $A_t\succeq0$ is fixed at the pre-update state and $d_t$
contains known momentum and weight-decay terms. We never call
$\widetilde\Delta$ the exact AdamW update. In particular, its raw-gradient
linearity omits global clipping; clipping discrepancy is measured as part of
optimizer-surrogate error.

To reduce storage, a fresh Rademacher projection
$R_t\in\{\pm1/\sqrt{k}\}^{k\times p}$ stores
$u_{c,t}=R_t\nabla L_c(\theta_t)$ and
$q_{i,t}=R_tA_t\nabla\ell_i$. A plan aggregates
\begin{equation}
q_{B,t}=\frac1{N_B}\sum_{i\in B}q_{i,t},\qquad v_t=R_td_t,
\end{equation}
and uses
\begin{equation}
\widehat S_{c,t}(B)=-u_{c,t}^\top v_t+
\eta_tu_{c,t}^\top q_{B,t}.
\label{eq:sketch-score}
\end{equation}
This includes response-token weighting and the candidate-independent optimizer
term. The projection reduces matching cost, not the backward passes needed to
obtain individual gradients, and any claim applies
only to the trainable subspace used for the sketches.

\subsection{Data Construction}

The candidate pool preserves source labels, license metadata, tokenized
lengths, and near-duplicate clusters. Candidate sampling uses a fixed
source-coverage floor so that the scheduler cannot permanently starve a source
before it has evidence to do so. Personally identifying fields, benchmark
solutions, and examples without a documented redistribution or research-use
basis are excluded before any selection method sees the pool.
Acquisition pins the repository revision and export-byte hash and records, for
every row, a stable upstream identifier plus hashes of the canonical raw row,
selected content, and a versioned Unicode-normalized form. Duplicate clusters
are formed before split assignment: a normalized cluster crossing registered
candidate-source, reference, or benchmark roles is quarantined in full, and a
retained cluster is assigned wholly to one downstream split.
Normalized equality is only an exact cleaning rule. A hash-bound preflight
then blocks named PII/secret patterns and retained character-5-gram
near-duplicates. This deterministic lexical screen is not a semantic or privacy
certificate: reviewed multilingual, paraphrase, benchmark-leakage, and privacy
audits remain separate blocking checks. Above the exhaustive limit, only a
reviewed exact engine whose hash-bound shards cover all unordered pairs can
lift the lexical blocker; the committed engine attestation is still unfrozen.

Reference sets measure losses rather than benchmark exact match because the
gate requires differentiability. For each capability, score, gate, audit, and
benchmark splits are disjoint at the source-template and semantic-cluster
levels. The scheduling sets are explicitly treated as training resources:
repeatedly querying $V^{\mathrm{score}}$ can overfit it, which is why no final
claim uses that set.

\paragraph{Frozen operational capability map.}
The pilot defines three dataset slices: general instruction, mathematics, and
code. In the mathematics setting, mathematics is the target and general
instruction plus code are the two protected slices; in the code setting, code
is the target and general instruction plus mathematics are protected. Every
slice has its own score, gate, and untouched audit partition with the same loss
definition. BigCodeBench is only a secondary code diagnostic and never a
differentiable gate loss. These labels are operational dataset constructs, not
complete capabilities.

Split assignment is frozen before losses are computed. Each complete reviewed
semantic-near-duplicate cluster is hashed with the source key, pinned source
revision, semantic-audit hash, and sorted member record identifiers.
Digest-mass intervals assign clusters to candidate/score/gate/audit with
bucket widths $0.80/0.05/0.10/0.05$; these are not forced finite-sample
row proportions. An empty required source partition blocks the pilot; no
boundary moves after labels or content are inspected. This gives
reproducible cluster-preserving assignment, not evidence that the reviewed
clusters capture every semantic overlap.

\subsection{Implementation}

For each proposed plan, \cref{eq:sketch-score} yields an estimated score. We
subtract separately calibrated margins for projection, reference sampling,
optimizer-surrogate error, and staleness:
\begin{equation}
\underline S_{c,t}(B)=\widehat S_{c,t}(B)-e_{c,t}(B).
\end{equation}
These margins are called empirical prediction margins unless their coverage
conditions are verified; an ensemble standard deviation is not a confidence
bound.
Because maximizing a noisy score creates selection bias, the beam returns a
fixed-size shortlist. An independent reference minibatch and projection,
unseen during search, re-score that shortlist. We report simultaneous
shortlist coverage; absent that evidence, ``lower bound'' is replaced by
``penalized score.''

The execution-cost reserve is a profiled prediction plus a margin calibrated
on independent plans. We call it an upper bound only when its simultaneous
coverage target is met; otherwise it is a budget predictor and the
confirmatory run cannot start. Step-norm error is bounded as
$\overline q_t=\widehat q_t+e_t^q$. The ideal round-level decision is
\begin{align}
\max_{B,\pi}\quad &\underline S_{0,t}(B) \label{eq:selection}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&\underline S_{0,t}(B)\ge0,\nonumber\\
&\underline S_{c,t}(B)\ge
\tfrac{\overline\beta_{c,t}}{2}\overline q_t(B)-\tau_{c,t},
\quad c=1{:}C,\nonumber\\
&\overline q_t(B)\le \rho_t^2,\qquad
\overline C_t^{\mathrm{all}}(B,\pi)\le B_t^{\mathrm{rem}},\nonumber\\
&N_{\min}\le N_B\le N_{\max},\quad
|\operatorname{range}(\pi)|\le P,\nonumber\\
&\text{\cref{eq:packing} holds,}\nonumber\\
&\text{and source-coverage constraints hold.}\nonumber
\end{align}
Here $\overline q_t$ estimates a trust-radius bound on
$\|\widetilde\Delta_t\|^2$, $\tau_{c,t}$ is an allowed one-step reference-loss
increase, and $B_t^{\mathrm{rem}}$ reserves the worst-case gate cost before
selection. A no-op plan is always feasible. The binary selection,
packing, norm, and non-additive cost terms make \cref{eq:selection} NP-hard.
Our beam search retains the best partial plans by cost and pack-count buckets,
then performs drop--swap repair. We claim no approximation ratio and compare
against an exact small-pool oracle.
A separate null action has $\Delta=0$, zero score and norm, and no optimizer
step or moment increment; it is not \cref{eq:train-loss} with $N_B=0$.
Actual allocated accelerator-seconds are metered. A reserve overrun is retained
as a protocol failure; before sealed audits open, every paired method is
restarted from the common initial state under a newly frozen larger reserve.

\paragraph{Actual-update gate.}
After one backward pass on the selected window, form the single post-unscale,
post-reduction gradient used by the frozen optimizer,
\begin{equation}
\overline G_B=G_B\min\{1,g_{\max}/\|G_B\|_2\},
\end{equation}
with the multiplier defined as one when $G_B=0$ and
$g_{\max}=\infty$ denoting no clipping. We construct AdamW moments
$m^+=\beta_1m+(1-\beta_1)\overline G_B$ and
$v^+=\beta_2v+(1-\beta_2)\overline G_B^{\odot2}$ without committing them. Bias-corrected
moments define direction
$a_t=-\widehat m^+/(\sqrt{\widehat v^+}+\epsilon)-\lambda\theta_t$ and candidate
displacement $\Delta_t(\alpha)=\alpha\eta_ta_t$. For the finite grid
$\mathcal A=\{2^{-j}:j=0,\ldots,J\}$, fresh gate examples evaluate
\begin{equation}
D_{c,j}(\alpha)=
\phi_c(z_j;\theta_t+\Delta_t(\alpha))-
\phi_c(z_j;\theta_t).
\end{equation}
Here $\phi_c$ is example-average NLL clipped to $[0,M_\phi]$ solely for the
gate. For each protected slice $c$, one fresh block is drawn with replacement
from its frozen gate law before inspecting any scale. Blocks are independent
across slices and rounds, while every $\alpha\in\mathcal A$ shares the same
paired block within $(c,t)$. With $n_c$ examples per block and at most
$T_{\max}$ rounds, define
\begin{equation}
U_{c,t}(\alpha)=\overline D_{c,t}(\alpha)+
M_\phi\sqrt{\frac{2\log(C|\mathcal A|T_{\max}/\delta_g)}{n_c}}.
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
The union-bound interpretation requires fixed $T_{\max}$ and fresh gate draws;
otherwise \cref{eq:gate} is only a heuristic. We commit the largest $\alpha$
with $U_{c,t}(\alpha)\le\tau_{c,t}$ for all protected slices, applying learning
rate $\alpha\eta_t$ while committing $m^+,v^+$ and the step counter exactly
once. If none passes, all parameter, optimizer, and scheduler state is restored
and the charged update is discarded. Target improvement remains a planning
criterion; it is not certified unless $c=0$ is included in the gate.
For each protected slice, a predeclared ledger enforces
$\sum_{t\le T}\tau_{c,t}\le b_c$. The single global $\delta_g$ in
\cref{eq:gate} already union-bounds all slices, scales, and rounds. This
composition concerns only the clipped gate estimand. Final unclipped audit NLL
remains an empirical endpoint, not a consequence of the gate.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One Scheduling and Update Round}
\label{alg:scheduler}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Sample candidates within the remaining scoring budget.
\STATE Compute lengths, trainable-subspace gradients, and sketches.
\STATE Build feasible isolated pack plans with beam search.
\STATE Re-score a fixed shortlist on independent references and select.
\STATE Compute its exact gradient, moments, and scaled AdamW candidates.
\FOR{$c=1,\ldots,C$}
  \STATE Draw a fresh protected block $Z_{c,t}$; freeze it across scales.
\ENDFOR
\FOR{$\alpha\in\mathcal A$ from largest to smallest}
  \STATE Evaluate protected losses on all frozen round blocks.
  \IF{all upper statistics are below their tolerances}
    \STATE Commit $\Delta_t(\alpha)$ and optimizer state; \textbf{break}.
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Charge scoring, solving, gating, training, and rejection costs.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Conditional One-Step Control}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:control}
Suppose $L_c$ is $\beta_c$-smooth on the segment from $\theta_t$ to
$\theta_t+\Delta$. Assume jointly that
$-\nabla L_c(\theta_t)^\top\Delta\ge\widehat S_c-r_c$,
$\|\Delta\|^2\le\overline q$, and
$\beta_c\le\overline\beta_c$. If
\begin{equation}
\widehat S_c-r_c\ge
\tfrac{\overline\beta_c}{2}\overline q-\tau_c,
\end{equation}
then
$L_c(\theta_t+\Delta)-L_c(\theta_t)\le\tau_c$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is the descent lemma and appears in
\cref{app:reproducibility}. The proposition must use the actual displacement;
substituting \cref{eq:surrogate} invalidates it. It controls one specified
reference loss, not accuracy, harmlessness, population generalization, or
multiple steps without accumulated tolerances. The practical gate controls a
clipped empirical loss under its own conditions; it does not establish the
score, norm, and curvature event required by \cref{prop:control}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The confirmatory study plans three hypotheses. \textbf{H1}: joint
selection--packing gives a better target-loss/worst-protected-loss frontier than
the same score followed by best-fit packing. \textbf{H2}: any gain remains after
charging selector, gate, and rejected-update costs. \textbf{H3}: the
actual-update gate reduces audit regressions relative to proxy-only
constraints. Failure of any hypothesis is a substantive negative result.
Only the exploratory H1 mechanism factorial is frozen in the committed pilot;
H2, H3, and every confirmatory acceptance threshold remain planned rather than
predeclared.
The primary statistic is the paired difference in dominated hypervolume over a
fixed tolerance and budget grid with a fixed reference box. H1 succeeds only
if its simultaneous 95\% lower bound exceeds zero. H3 compares protected
regression at a planned noninferior target-NLL change so that shrinking all
updates cannot win trivially.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We freeze a source-labeled, license-audited instruction mixture before tuning.
Two target settings---mathematics and code---swap which source is targeted and
which capabilities are protected. Development uses a roughly 1--2B parameter
model; confirmatory runs use a roughly 7--8B model and one second model family.
The planned trainable configuration is rank-64 adapters on attention and MLP
projections, BF16 AdamW, response-only loss, $L_{\max}=4096$, and isolated
packing. The pilot freezes $P=8$ packs and microbatches per update and
$N_B\in[4096,8192]$ response tokens for every method. Exact model revisions,
tokenizer hashes, data hashes, optimizer
coefficients, and kernel versions must be frozen in the artifact before the
first confirmatory run.
The committed 20-update, 131,072-reference-token configuration is explicitly a
pre-compute pilot, not the $B_{50M}$ confirmatory design. Its gate uses 512
with-replacement draws per protected slice and a frozen empirical margin, with
$\sum_t\tau_{c,t}\le0.2$; it makes no population-coverage claim. A formal use
of \cref{eq:gate} requires a separately feasible $T_{\max}$ and recomputed
sample count.

Let $B_{50M}$ be the measured accelerator cost of random, isolated,
best-fit-packed SFT on 50 million response tokens. We evaluate total budgets
$\{0.25,0.5,1.0\}B_{50M}$. Each method stops at the same allocated
accelerator-second budget, including accelerator preprocessing and validation.
CPU-seconds and wall time are reported separately. Final common evaluation is
excluded from training cost; every method-specific query is included.

Baselines are random plus identical packing, representation-similarity
selection, LESS, FisherSFT, influence distillation, SPICE, DynamixSFT, a
source-level dynamic allocator, GIST, UDS, PartitionSel, P2D, online
target-gradient alignment followed by packing, and the proposed joint
scheduler. Every feasible baseline is reported
both ungated and with the identical actual-update gate. The decisive decoupled
comparator uses the same candidates, scores, protected constraints, and
training code, differing only by additive selection followed by deterministic
best-fit packing. Candidate streams, initial checkpoints, data order seeds, and
hyperparameter search budgets are paired. \Cref{tab:main} shows the core
methods; the appendix reports the complete baseline--gate crossing, including
influence distillation and the source-level allocator.
Best-fit, HBP, and SlimPack-style execution policies are crossed with a fixed
selector where their public implementations support the frozen models; this
comparison measures systems efficiency, not selection quality.

Primary endpoints are target audit NLL, the worst protected audit regression
\begin{equation}
\max_{c\ge1}
\left[L_c^{\mathrm{audit}}(\theta_T)-
L_c^{\mathrm{audit}}(\theta_0)\right],
\end{equation}
end-to-end allocated accelerator-seconds, CPU resource-seconds, wall time, and
packing throughput. Downstream benchmarks
are secondary because they are not optimized by the gate. We use five
development seeds and five confirmatory seeds, report paired differences with
95\% intervals, and retain every run rather than selecting the best seed.

\subsection{Ablations and Mechanistic Analysis}

Planned confirmatory one-factor contrasts compare offline versus online refresh,
score-then-pack versus joint planning, target-only versus protected constraints,
SGD geometry versus frozen-Adam geometry, additive token cost versus measured
cost, and proxy-only versus actual-update gating. A small fractional factorial
on the development model checks the largest interactions. Sketch dimensions
and refresh intervals are tuned only on that model.
The core $2\times2$ contrast crosses additive versus profiled selection cost
with deterministic best-fit versus beam-search packing, separating a better
cost model from a better packing heuristic. All four cells use identical
candidates, subset moves, beam width, expansion count, and wall-clock solver
budget. The primary mechanistic statistic is the paired
difference-in-differences in dominated hypervolume, supplemented by beam error
against exact 24-candidate instances. A second cross uses frozen-Adam
and GIST scores with the same packing paths.

Mechanistic diagnostics compare sketches with exact adapter-space dot
products, surrogate and actual AdamW displacements, first-order predictions and
realized gate losses, predicted and measured pack cost, solver plans and a
small-pool exact oracle, and score-set versus sealed-audit regressions. If
packing costs are effectively additive or joint plans match score-then-pack,
the packing-aware claim is removed.

\subsection{Robustness and Generalization}

Robustness tests transfer the frozen scheduler configuration across model size
and family, perturb source proportions, tighten each protected tolerance, and
replace reference templates with semantically matched held-out templates.
Numerical controls compare isolated packed and unpacked gradients with dropout
disabled. Contamination audits combine exact, normalized, and semantic
near-duplicate search between candidates, all references, and final benchmarks.
Results are disaggregated by source, length decile, language, and license class;
no subgroup conclusion is made where sample support is inadequate.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The method may spend more compute scoring and gating than it saves in training.
Its pack heuristic is neither optimal nor approximation-guaranteed. Frozen
Adam geometry can rank windows incorrectly, while computing individual
gradients may dominate runtime. Reference losses are incomplete proxies for
capabilities and can themselves be gamed; repeated score queries make them
training data. A gate based on finite fresh samples can miss rare regressions,
and formal simultaneous coverage may be too conservative to be useful.

The conditional bound is a standard local smoothness consequence, not a safety
theorem. It applies only to the measured trainable subspace and one actual
update. It does not control downstream metrics, generation behavior, or
long-horizon accumulation unless every tolerance and failure probability is
accounted for. Finally, evidence from open 1--8B models may not transfer to
full-parameter or frontier-scale training.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We defined a narrow test of whether data selection should be coupled to
physical packing when SFT must preserve specified reference losses under a
true compute budget. The proposed scheduler combines known gradient-proxy
ingredients with non-additive pack planning and a gate on the actual optimizer
displacement. The analysis clarifies what this construction can and cannot
guarantee. Whether the interaction is practically valuable remains an
empirical question; the decisive next step is the paired score-then-pack versus
joint-planning experiment with all overhead charged.

\section*{Impact Statement}

Compute-aware scheduling could reduce the resources needed to specialize
language models and make controlled adaptation accessible to smaller research
groups. The same efficiency can accelerate the creation of models optimized
for harmful applications. Protected losses may create false assurance if they
omit affected languages, communities, or failure modes, and optimization
against fixed references can shift harm outside the measured set. Candidate
data may contain private, copyrighted, or exploitative labor-derived material;
selection does not cure those provenance problems. We therefore require
license and privacy audits before scoring, disaggregated untouched audits,
transparent reporting of gate coverage, and no use of the phrase
``capability-safe.'' All selector and rejected-update compute is reported so
environmental benefits are not overstated.

\section{Reproducibility Details}

\subsection{Proof of \cref{prop:control}}
By $\beta_c$-smoothness along the update segment,
\begin{align}
L_c(\theta_t+\Delta)
&\le L_c(\theta_t)+\nabla L_c(\theta_t)^\top\Delta
  +\frac{\beta_c}{2}\|\Delta\|^2\\
&\le L_c(\theta_t)-(\widehat S_c-r_c)
  +\frac{\overline\beta_c}{2}\overline q\\
&\le L_c(\theta_t)+\tau_c.
\end{align}
The first inequality is deterministic. Any probabilistic claim enters only
through the joint event that the assumed score, norm, and curvature bounds
hold.

\subsection{Required Run Manifest}
Every run records the base checkpoint and revision, tokenizer hash, trainable
parameter names, optimizer and scheduler state, precision, gradient clipping,
random seeds, data and deduplication hashes, candidate stream, score/gate/audit
split identifiers, sketch seed and dimension, beam width, cost-model version,
packing order, kernel and compiler versions, hardware identifier, accepted and
rejected displacements, and cumulative accelerator time. A failed or
preempted run remains in the ledger.

\subsection{Gate Feasibility and Protocol Manifest}
Even when the observed mean change is zero, the gate in \cref{eq:gate}
requires approximately
\begin{equation}
n_c\ge
\frac{2M_\phi^2\log(C|\mathcal A|T_{\max}/\delta_g)}
{\tau_{c,t}^2}.
\end{equation}
Any formal confirmatory configuration must freeze
$C,M_\phi,J,T_{\max},\delta_g,b_c$, every
$\tau_{c,t}$ and $n_c$, the sampling seeds, and disjoint $(c,t)$ gate blocks,
with $\sum_{t\le T_{\max}}\tau_{c,t}\le b_c$. It records the derived radius
and total required examples. If $\widehat c_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ is the measured
per-example forward cost, the round reserves
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{gate}}=\widehat c_{\mathrm{fwd}}
(|\mathcal A|+1)\sum_{c=1}^{C}n_c.
\end{equation}
No round starts unless scoring, candidate-update, and worst-case gate reserves
all fit the remaining budget. If this is infeasible, the simultaneous-control
condition is removed and any cheaper gate is labeled heuristic; H3 then remains
an empirical gate comparison only. The repository protocol runner
\texttt{python3 scripts/run\_experiment\_pipeline.py
--plan experiments/sft\_pipeline.json} enumerates acquisition, processing,
model construction, 20-update exploratory training, and sealed pilot evaluation
artifacts. The full $B_{50M}$ confirmatory configuration is not yet frozen.
It hashes the exact SFT section of
\texttt{experiments/pilot\_preregistration.json}, including the fixed
eight-pack window, optimizer, selector, gate, cost-failure rule, and factorial
contrast. Its software audit independently enumerates $(B,\pi)$ pairs and,
separately, minimizes a numeric nonlinear pack cost over $\pi$ before selecting
$B$; equality is therefore not implemented by calling the same subset
predicate twice. The public-source registry pins full dataset and checkpoint commits;
its automated license metadata check is only a preflight, and real acquisition
is blocked until every manual provenance, terms, privacy, and leakage review is
approved.
After approval, \texttt{scripts/materialize\_public\_data.py} accepts only
hash-pinned local JSONL exports, emits row-level provenance and retained
role-specific files, and verifies their artifact hashes. Its committed
synthetic fixture contains within-role and cross-role duplicates and succeeds
only when the former is collapsed and the latter is quarantined; it downloads
no public rows and is not experimental evidence.
\texttt{scripts/run\_content\_gates.py} additionally binds
\texttt{experiments/content\_gates.json} to those artifacts and fails closed on
retained named PII/secret patterns or lexical near-duplicates. Its exhaustive
small-artifact path can pass; the non-recall-complete MinHash-LSH path for
larger artifacts is inconclusive by itself.
\texttt{scripts/validate\_external\_overlap\_audit.py} now defines the only
large-artifact completion route: records are ordered by identifier, unordered
pairs receive row-major upper-triangle ordinals, and contiguous shard ranges
must cover exactly $n(n-1)/2$ comparisons without gaps or overlaps. Each shard
binds the materialization, gate specification, reviewed engine source, runtime
image, and review record; approximation, early termination, shard errors, or
any qualifying pair blocks the ledger. Reported pair identities, roles,
thresholds, and scores are independently checked without persisting matched
text. A temporary disk-backed exact join also enumerates every pair sharing a
5-gram and rejects omitted findings; this is recall-complete for the positive
frozen Jaccard thresholds because any qualifying pair must share a gram. Shard
coverage is therefore an engine-governance attestation, whereas readiness
depends on reproducing the complete qualifying-pair set. The committed engine
hashes remain null blockers, so this contract is executable only as a generated
software self-test and no large real artifact currently passes. Neither lexical
route is evidence of semantic independence or completed manual review.
The separate \texttt{experiments/sft\_reference\_contract.json} fixes the
capability map and domain-separated cluster-hash split. The process-stage
validator reproduces every cluster identifier, assignment digest, bucket, and
partition from retained record identifiers and the reviewed semantic-audit
hash. Its generated self-test moves one cluster to a wrong partition,
duplicates one cluster, and stales the contract binding; all three must be
rejected. The self-test contains identifiers only and is not empirical
evidence.
With \texttt{--execute --synthetic-audit --commands
experiments/synthetic\_commands.json}, a
CPU-only audit exactly enumerates packings for two length-10 packs under a
quadratic load plus launch cost, checks direct joint selection against an
independently computed profiled-cost form, and exercises the
finite gate grid over 512 seeded score perturbations. A separate frozen
Hoeffding fixture uses $M_\phi=1$, $C=2$, $|\mathcal A|=3$,
$T_{\max}=10$, $\delta_g=0.05$, and $\tau=0.2$, which fixes
$n_c=355$. Every output is marked non-empirical. These fixtures test
implementation invariants and a constructed non-additive-cost case; they are
not evidence that the method improves SFT.
\texttt{scripts/aggregate\_sft\_factorial.py} separately implements the frozen
reference-box clipping, failed-run assignment, dominated hypervolume,
cost-by-packer difference-in-differences, and paired max-$t$ seed bootstrap.
Its self-test uses only generated endpoints and cannot populate
\cref{tab:main}.

\subsection{Numerical Packing Check}
Before training, randomly sampled windows are evaluated both unpacked and with
isolated packing. We compare response-token counts, losses, and every
trainable-parameter gradient. A discrepancy above a predeclared numerical
tolerance blocks the experiment rather than being absorbed into scheduler
noise.
