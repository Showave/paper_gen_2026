\section{Evaluation Method}
\label{sec:method}

\placeholder{Specify task generation or sampling, response collection,
scoring, aggregation, missing-data handling, and uncertainty estimation.
Separate choices required by the construct from conveniences of the
implementation. State which conclusions each score does and does not support.}

\subsection{Data and Task Construction}

\placeholder{Document source populations, sampling frames, inclusion and
exclusion rules, licenses, deduplication, contamination controls, annotation
protocols, quality checks, and versioning. Report category distributions and
known coverage gaps. Preserve a genuinely held-out test set.}

\subsection{Scoring and Aggregation}

\placeholder{Define scores mathematically, including normalization, weighting,
ties, abstentions, invalid outputs, and multiple attempts. Justify aggregation
against the intended use and report disaggregated results whenever an average
can hide important failures.}

\subsection{Human or Model-Based Judging}

\placeholder{Specify rubrics, rater recruitment and training, blinding,
randomization, adjudication, judge prompts and versions, position-bias
controls, and agreement measures. Never treat agreement with a model judge as
ground truth without independent validation.}

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\placeholder{Present evidence for construct, content, criterion, and
convergent validity as applicable. Measure test--retest and inter-rater
reliability, sensitivity to meaningful system differences, and invariance to
nuisance transformations. Predefine acceptable thresholds where possible.}

\subsection{Contamination and Gaming Resistance}

\placeholder{Audit overlap with training-accessible sources using more than
exact string matching. Test prompt leakage, memorization, evaluator
overfitting, judge manipulation, and benchmark-specific optimization. Explain
the refresh or secure-evaluation plan.}

\subsection{Statistical Protocol}

\placeholder{Define the sampling unit, estimand, confidence interval or test,
multiple-comparison treatment, power or precision target, and ranking
stability analysis. Use paired comparisons when systems answer the same
items, and avoid treating items as independent when clusters exist.}

\section{Empirical Study}
\label{sec:results}

\placeholder{Select systems to span relevant capabilities, architectures,
access levels, and release dates without turning the paper into a leaderboard.
Fix model versions, prompts, sampling settings, and budgets. Use the study to
test the evaluation method's claims rather than to market individual systems.}

\subsection{Main Findings}

\placeholder{Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not ranks alone. Include
per-category outcomes, ties, invalid-response rates, and sensitivity to
reasonable scoring choices. Distinguish confirmatory findings from
post-hoc observations.}

\subsection{Robustness and Ablations}

\placeholder{Vary prompts, judges, task subsets, aggregation rules, and random
seeds. Remove each design component and test whether conclusions survive
plausible nuisance changes. Analyze disagreement cases rather than discarding
them.}
