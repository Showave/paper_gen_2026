\begin{abstract}
Pairwise language-model evaluations increasingly use inexpensive model judges,
but deployment decisions concern a specified human population and must account
for ties, prompt variation, correlated judge errors, and adaptively chosen
human labels. We formulate decision-calibrated paired evaluation around the
finite-frame estimand ``human win probability minus loss probability'' under
fixed item, prompt, response-generation, rater, and display-order
distributions. A tie-aware hierarchical jury model drives one-step
value-of-information acquisition, while an explicit random-exploration mixture
and logged draw probabilities preserve design-based identification. A
prequential augmented Hansen--Hurwitz estimator is conditionally unbiased
under stated response-stability, positivity, and boundedness conditions and
supports fixed-budget martingale inference even when the jury model is
misspecified. This
methods-first draft specifies simulation, human-audit, and temporal-shift tests;
efficiency and decision-quality claims remain pending empirical execution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Model comparison is often reduced to a leaderboard score, although the actual
decision may be whether system $A$ should replace system $B$ for a particular
population of requests. Human pairwise evaluation directly targets preference
and underlies Chatbot Arena \cite{chiang2024arena}, but comprehensive human
labeling is expensive. Model-based judges scale cheaply
\cite{zheng2023judging} yet exhibit position, style, verbosity, and
self-preference effects \cite{panickssery2024evaluators,ye2025justice}. A jury
of correlated judges cannot be treated as an equally large set of independent
humans.

Recent work estimates judge-specific bias and uncertainty
\cite{dubois2026biases}, shared confounding \cite{zhao2026care}, jury
discrimination and internal consistency \cite{qian2026jury}, and uncertainty
with iterative comparison selection in comparative evaluation
\cite{fathullah2025uncertainty}. Human-calibrated estimators correct noisy
model judgments \cite{lee2026report,chen2026efficient}. Efficient inference for
a static, independently selected calibration set is now well understood enough
that ``apply AIPW to judge labels'' is not a new contribution.

The specific issue studied here is the combination of a tie-aware crossed
human-preference frame, decision-loss acquisition, and sequential inference.
Once human cells are chosen because previous outcomes made them
decision-relevant, a final inclusion probability cannot generally be
reconstructed by multiplying probabilities along the realized path. Ordinary
post-hoc cross-fitting can leak future selection information, while pure
value-of-information acquisition can assign zero probability to target cells.

We ask: \emph{can adaptive human labeling reduce the cost of deciding whether
$A$ is materially better than $B$ while retaining valid inference for an
explicit human-preference estimand?} We make four scoped contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We define a tie-aware finite evaluation frame that fixes item, prompt,
  response coupling, rater, and display-order distributions before labeling.
  \item We use a crossed jury model only for prediction and decision-targeted
  acquisition, not as the definition of quality.
  \item We combine value-of-information acquisition with randomized exploration
  and a draw-level sequential augmented Hansen--Hurwitz estimator. We state
  its response-and-design assumptions and fixed-budget asymptotic conditions.
  \item We lay out planned simulations, a sealed target-random human audit,
    shift tests, and ablations that separate interval validity from label
    efficiency; executable DGPs remain a preregistration blocker.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Evidence status.}
The formal design claims are established below. Whether adaptive acquisition
saves labels, whether the working model predicts humans, and whether the chosen
human panel represents deployment stakeholders remain empirical questions; no
such result is reported in this draft.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Measurement variation and judge bias.}
Multi-prompt evaluation models performance distributions across prompt forms
\cite{polo2024multiprompt}. Human studies can be assessed by the probability
that a repeat study preserves a ranking \cite{riley2024replicable}. Model
judges introduce additional structure: self-recognition can produce
self-preference \cite{panickssery2024evaluators}, controlled perturbations
reveal multiple biases \cite{ye2025justice}, and modern hierarchical methods
estimate grader effects, confounding, or discrimination
\cite{dubois2026biases,zhao2026care,qian2026jury}. Our jury model borrows this
measurement perspective. Its novelty is not a new bias taxonomy or latent
quality model.

\paragraph{Human-calibrated inference.}
Binary sensitivity/specificity correction with propagated calibration
uncertainty and pilot-based allocation across true-label classes is studied by
\citet{lee2026report}. \citet{chen2026efficient} unify measurement-error and
prediction-powered estimators for mean human outcomes, including pairwise win
rates, under independently selected calibration/test designs. Those estimators
do not by themselves justify reusing an outcome-adaptively acquired
calibration sample. Classical unequal-probability
with-replacement estimation \cite{hansen1943sampling} and inference for
adaptively collected data \cite{hadad2021confidence} supply the statistical
foundation. Prediction-Powered Active Testing (PPAT) combines
active-testing weights with a prediction control variate
\cite{caron2026ppat}; Active Testing supplies the earlier adaptive risk
estimation framework \cite{kossen2021active}, while control-variate evaluation
combines synthetic and human feedback for unbiased head-to-head win rates
\cite{zhou2025synthetic}. Our distinction is decision-loss acquisition for
three-way human comparisons over a crossed evaluation frame, not adaptive
residualization or its central limit theorem by itself.
DMLRank gives nonparametric, debiased inference for tie-aware preference
rankings and derives budget-aware collection policies
\cite{frauen2026dmlrank}. Its target is a multi-model ranking functional; ours
is one frozen replacement decision for an explicitly weighted
item--response--rater--order frame, with outcome-adaptive draw probabilities
logged prequentially.

\paragraph{Adaptive and dynamic evaluation.}
Active acquisition can exploit historical dependence among benchmark items
\cite{li2025active}, while ProEval uses surrogate models for performance
estimation and failure discovery \cite{huang2026proeval}. Such compression can
fail under source--target model shift \cite{zhang2025fewer}. Dynamic benchmarks
reduce static contamination through generated or refreshed tasks
\cite{zhu2024dyval,white2025livebench}; contamination tests can detect some
forms of memorized ordering \cite{oren2024contamination}. Freshness, however,
changes the evaluation distribution and complicates longitudinal claims.
Finally, non-transitive judge preferences can make a scalar rank
baseline-dependent \cite{xu2025nontransitivity}. We therefore target one
prespecified pairwise replacement decision, not a universal leaderboard.
SCOPE conformally calibrates abstention for pairwise model judgments under
exchangeability \cite{badshah2026scope}; its selective-judge risk is distinct
from estimating a weighted human-panel preference with adaptive labels.
Collaborative adaptive labeling also combines imperfect labels with selective
expert escalation and dependence-aware estimation
\cite{ruan2026collaborative}. FairJudge instead trains the judge itself to
reduce selected biases \cite{yang2026fairjudge}. Our model jury remains
fallible: identification comes from randomized human acquisition, and the
claimed novelty is limited to the tie-aware crossed decision frame and
decision-loss policy.
AURA adaptively prioritizes uncertain pairwise judge decisions for human
verification \cite{zhang2026aura}. It targets refinement of latent judge trust;
our target is instead a prespecified weighted human-panel mean, with the
acquisition model allowed to be wrong.

\section{Evaluation Goal and Desiderata}
\label{sec:goal}

At evaluation time $t_0$, freeze models $A,B$, their decoding policies, the
rubric, and a finite frame of $N$ root items. Item $i$ has target weight
$w_i\ge0$, $\sum_iw_i=1$. Cell $c\in\mathcal C_i$ identifies a prompt variant
and paired response realizations with prespecified conditional weight
$a_{c\mid i}$. Independent response draws, shared random seeds, and
deterministic decoding define different estimands because preference is
nonlinear; the coupling is therefore fixed explicitly.

Before acquisition, enroll a finite rater panel
$\mathcal H=\{1,\ldots,H\}$ with nonnegative target weights $\rho_h$ summing to
one. Inference therefore targets this panel; extrapolation to other people is a
separate superpopulation claim. For display order $o\in\{-1,+1\}$, define the
outcome of one standardized rating draw
\begin{equation}
Y_{icho}=
\begin{cases}
+1,&A\text{ preferred},\\
0,&\text{tie},\\
-1,&B\text{ preferred}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and $\mu_{icho}=\mathbb E[Y_{icho}\mid i,c,h,o]$, where the expectation covers
within-rater response variation under the fixed protocol.
The finite-frame target is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_F=\sum_{i=1}^Nw_i\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_i}a_{c\mid i}
\sum_{h\in\mathcal H}\rho_h\frac12\sum_{o\in\{-1,+1\}}\mu_{icho}.
\label{eq:estimand}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, define a target unit $x=(i,c,h,o)\in\mathcal X$ with mass
\begin{equation}
\pi(x)=w_i a_{c\mid i}\rho_h/2,\qquad \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\pi(x)=1.
\label{eq:target-mass}
\end{equation}
Then $\Delta_F=P_\pi(A\text{ wins})-P_\pi(B\text{ wins})$ and the half-tie win
rate is $(1+\Delta_F)/2$. We separately target
\begin{equation}
\tau_F=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\pi(x)P(Y_x=0\mid x)
\end{equation}
because the same $\Delta_F$ can describe decisive disagreement or widespread
ties.

A practical margin $\delta>0$ and loss matrix are pre-specified. The action set
is ``$A$ materially better'' if $\Delta_F>\delta$, ``practically equivalent''
if $|\Delta_F|\le\delta$, ``$B$ materially better'' if
$\Delta_F<-\delta$, and ``not established'' when data do not support a
decisive action. This is an operational human-preference construct, not latent
universal quality.

\section{Evaluation Method}
\label{sec:method}

The protocol first collects inexpensive labels from a family-diverse model
jury on all cells, then an IID target-random human pilot from $\pi$. Before each
subsequent batch, a working model is trained only on prior batches and used to
compute decision value. Human acquisition mixes this adaptive policy with the
target distribution. A sealed IID target-random human audit is never
used for fitting or acquisition.
The implementation refits once per predeclared batch. It approximates each
hypothetical EVI update by one frozen Laplace/Newton step, forms one $p_b$ for
the next batch, and logs that probability for every draw. The working
Bayes-risk action used inside EVI is distinct from the terminal confidence-set
action. Conditional on the batch-start history, draws are IID with replacement
from this frozen law and the predictor is fixed; draw-level \cref{eq:psi}
remains valid because both are predictable.

\subsection{Data and Task Construction}

The frame is a probability sample from a timestamped deployment log or a
weighted benchmark with a clearly narrower finite-frame interpretation.
Sampling excludes personal data, unsupported licenses, and items requiring
professional expertise absent from the rater pool. Root-item provenance,
inclusion weight, domain, language, safety category, near-duplicate cluster,
prompt generator, response seeds, model revisions, tokenizer, and decoding
parameters are versioned before labels are revealed.
Each public root also records its pinned repository revision, export-byte hash,
stable upstream identifier, canonical raw-row hash, and versioned
normalized-content hash. Normalized duplicate clusters are formed before frame
sampling and assigned as units; any separately admitted pilot-fitting or
sealed source is a distinct registered role, and cross-role clusters are
quarantined. Paraphrase and template leakage remain separate blocking checks,
since hash inequality is not evidence of semantic independence. A hash-bound
preflight additionally rejects named PII/secret patterns and retained
character-5-gram near-duplicates; it is a lexical heuristic, not a substitute
for reviewed multilingual, paraphrase, privacy, or benchmark-leakage audits.
Above the exhaustive limit, only a reviewed exact engine whose hash-bound
shards cover every unordered pair can lift the lexical blocker; the committed
engine attestation is still unfrozen.

Each prompt variant is validated as meaning-preserving by a separate process;
the target weights over variants are fixed, not chosen after seeing which model
benefits. Model identifiers are hidden (although style may reveal identity),
AB/BA display is randomized, and humans
receive a real tie option with a fixed definition. ``Equally good'' and ``both
unacceptable'' are recorded separately even if the primary analysis maps both
to zero. Independent ratings define \cref{eq:estimand}; majority vote and
adjudication are secondary, different estimands.

Rater and order assignments follow the target masses in
\cref{eq:target-mass}, or their logged adaptive counterparts. Availability is
resolved before content is shown. Every assignment attempt is logged; a skip
after viewing content is a separate outcome, never silently reassigned. The
formal estimator below applies only to complete ratings. For nonresponse we
report worst-case bounds and a separately labeled inverse-response analysis
under a stated missing-at-random model; outcome-dependent skipping is not
repaired by weighting alone.

\subsection{Scoring and Aggregation}

At round $t$, sample one complete target unit with replacement,
\begin{equation}
X_t=(I_t,C_t,H_t,O_t)\sim
p_t(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ contains every prior assignment, sampling
probability, jury feature, outcome, and model random seed. This joint law makes
rater and order assignment explicit. If operational constraints require a
nested assignment, the product of logged item, cell, rater, and order
probabilities must equal $p_t(X_t)$.
Write $Y_t^\star$ for the standardized rating that would be recorded under a
completed assignment. The design claim additionally requires stability of its
full response law:
\begin{equation}
P(Y_t^\star=y\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},X_t=x)=p_y(x),
\quad y\in\{-1,0,1\},
\label{eq:response-stability}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_x=\sum_yyp_y(x)$.
Learning, fatigue, memory, or rubric drift can violate
\cref{eq:response-stability}. Randomized task order, training before study
labels, and temporal diagnostics reduce but do not prove this assumption.
The protocol randomizes attempt positions, interleaves hidden audit attempts,
and retains repeated roots rather than resampling them. A sensitivity analysis
assumes the weaker uniform bound
$|\mathbb E[Y_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},X_t=x]-\mu_x|\le\gamma$;
then the design estimator's absolute drift bias is at most $\gamma$, so both
interval endpoints are expanded by the frozen $\gamma\in\{0,.02,.05\}$.

Let $m_{t-1}(x)\in[-1,1]$ be a bounded prediction of $Y_x$ trained only on
prior rounds and frozen before draw $t$, and define
\begin{equation}
\bar m_{t-1}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\pi(x)m_{t-1}(x).
\end{equation}
The draw-level sequential augmented Hansen--Hurwitz contribution is
\begin{equation}
\psi_t=\bar m_{t-1}+
\frac{\pi(X_t)}{p_t(X_t)}
\{Y_{X_t}-m_{t-1}(X_t)\},
\label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
with final estimator
\begin{equation}
\widehat\Delta_T=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\psi_t.
\label{eq:estimator}
\end{equation}
Repeated draws are retained. $p_t$ is the conditional draw probability, not
the probability that a unit is ever included; substituting a realized-path
inclusion probability is generally invalid under outcome adaptation. Replacing
$Y_x$ in \cref{eq:psi} by $\mathbf{1}\{Y_x=0\}$ gives the tie-rate estimator,
with its covariance with $\widehat\Delta_T$ reported.

The fixed $T$ counts assignment attempts, never replacement completions. Let
$R_t$ indicate observation and store $Y_t^{\rm obs}=R_tY_t^\star$, where
$Y_t^\star\in[-1,1]$; let $\psi_t^\star$ denote \cref{eq:psi} with this
complete outcome. The primary report replaces a missing outcome pointwise by
$-1$ and $+1$ in
\begin{align}
\psi_t^\pm
&=\bar m_{t-1}+\frac{\pi(X_t)}{p_t(X_t)}
\left[R_t\{Y_t^{\rm obs}-m_{t-1}(X_t)\}\right.\nonumber\\
&\qquad\left.{}+(1-R_t)\{\pm1-m_{t-1}(X_t)\}\right].
\end{align}
Thus $\psi_t^-\le\psi_t^\star\le\psi_t^+$ for every assignment, without a
missing-at-random assumption. Exact interval construction appears in
\cref{sec:statistical-protocol}. A separate analysis assumes
$P(R_t=1\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},X_t,Y_t^\star)=e_t(X_t)\ge e_{\min}>0$ and uses
the predictable contribution
\[
\psi_t^{\rm MAR}=\bar m_{t-1}+
\frac{\pi(X_t)}{p_t(X_t)}
\frac{R_t}{e_t(X_t)}
\{Y_t^{\rm obs}-m_{t-1}(X_t)\}.
\]
It is secondary because an estimated response model does not make
outcome-dependent skipping ignorable.

\subsection{Human or Model-Based Judging}

The jury model is a prediction and diagnostic instrument, not the source of
identification. A covariate-dependent generalization of the Davidson tie model
\cite{davidson1970ties} uses
\begin{align}
D_{jico}&=e^{\eta_{jico}/2}+e^{-\eta_{jico}/2}
+e^{\kappa_{jico}},\\
P_j(Y=1)&=e^{\eta_{jico}/2}/D_{jico},\\
P_j(Y=-1)&=e^{-\eta_{jico}/2}/D_{jico},\\
P_j(Y=0)&=e^{\kappa_{jico}}/D_{jico}.
\end{align}
One explicit working predictor is
\begin{align}
\eta_{jico}&=\alpha_j+d_j(\beta+b_i+b_{iv})
+\gamma_j^Oo+\gamma_j^L\ell_{ic}\nonumber\\
&\quad+\gamma_j^Ss_{jic}+u_{j,d(i)},\\
\kappa_{jico}&=\kappa_j+t_i+t_{iv}+z_{ic}^{\top}\xi_j,
\end{align}
with $d_j>0$ and partially pooled effects. Identifiability constraints set
weighted human-panel offset to zero and weighted log-discrimination to zero,
center item and variant effects, and require a connected model-judge--cell
overlap graph with both orders and estimable covariate contrasts. The public
pilot has one checkpoint per jury family, so it does not estimate a separate
family-variance component. Human-rater effects are shrinkage-only working
predictions under a proper prior; the 200-attempt pilot does not support a
pilot-era rater-heterogeneity claim or require every rater to appear.
Conditional independence is only a working assumption. The two recorded tie
subtypes are collapsed to zero before this three-category model and analyzed
separately.

The human scale is anchored by the frozen weighted panel rather than assuming
jury consistency equals validity. Length and family coefficients remain
descriptive unless randomized interventions identify them: removing a length
association can remove genuine quality as well as bias.

\paragraph{Decision-targeted acquisition.}
The protocol fixes the normalized loss matrix below, with rows
$(A,B,E,\mathrm{NE})$ and true states $(A,B,E)$:
\begin{equation}
L=\begin{pmatrix}
0&4&2\\4&0&2\\2&2&0\\1&1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Stakeholder-specific alternatives are sensitivity analyses, not post-hoc
replacements. Let $\vartheta$ collect the finite working-model coefficients.
At batch start $b$, freeze the Laplace posterior
$q_b(\vartheta)=\mathcal N(\widehat\vartheta_b,H_b^{-1})$, the deterministic
frame functional $\Delta(\vartheta)$, and
\begin{align}
Q_b(S=s)&=\int\mathbf 1\{S(\Delta(\vartheta))=s\}
q_b(\vartheta)d\vartheta,\\
\widehat p_b(y\mid x)&=\int p_\vartheta(y\mid x)q_b(\vartheta)d\vartheta.
\end{align}
Both integrals use frozen common Monte Carlo draws. Define Bayes risk
\begin{equation}
\widetilde R(Q)=
\min_{d\in\{A,B,E,\mathrm{NE}\}}
\sum_s L_{ds}Q(S=s),
\end{equation}
breaking action ties in the fixed order $\mathrm{NE},E,A,B$. Let
$q_b^{x,y}$ be obtained from $q_b$ by one predeclared damped Newton update of
the mode and observed Hessian using $\log p_\vartheta(y\mid x)$, with frozen
prior, ridge, damping, and no-line-search rule. Then $U(Q_b;x,y)$ is the state
distribution induced by $q_b^{x,y}$ through the same
$\Delta(\vartheta)$ and common draws. Neither the update nor its
hyperparameters depend on $p_b$. The update evaluates gradient and Hessian at
$\widehat\vartheta_b$, uses the frozen ridge/damping formula in the
preregistration, and factorizes with a fixed jitter ladder. Standard-normal
draws use seed 2026090322. If every registered Cholesky attempt fails, that
cell receives EVI zero and the failure is retained rather than silently
changing the approximation. For target unit $x$ with annotation cost
$k_x$, define
\begin{align}
\operatorname{EVI}_{b}(x)
&=\widetilde R(Q_b)\nonumber\\
&\quad-
\sum_{y\in\{-1,0,1\}}\widehat p_b(y\mid x)
\widetilde R\{U(Q_b;x,y)\}.
\end{align}
At each adaptive batch start, draw a frozen $K=256$ candidate multiset
$\mathcal C_b$ IID with replacement from $\pi$, before computing EVI. Let
$n_b(x)$ be its multiplicity. Using predeclared temperature $\lambda_E$, define
\begin{align}
g_b(x)&=\lambda_E\operatorname{EVI}_{b}(x)/k_x,\\
r_b(x)&=\frac{n_b(x)\exp\{g_b(x)\}}
{\sum_{x'}n_b(x')\exp\{g_b(x')\}},\\
p_b(x)&=\epsilon\pi(x)+(1-\epsilon)r_b(x),
\qquad \epsilon>0.
\label{eq:explore}
\end{align}
Here $n_b(x)=0$ sets $r_b(x)=0$; duplicates are retained through their
multiplicity. A domain-separated seed schedule and log-sum-exp implementation
are frozen. Across 40 adaptive batches this caps computation at 30,720
hypothetical Newton updates and 125,829,120 posterior-draw evaluations.
All EVI values are computed before $p_b$, after which $Q_b,p_b,m_b$, and
$\bar m_b$ are frozen for the complete batch; each draw-level $p_t$ equals
that $p_b$. This removes any dependence of EVI on its own acquisition
probability and gives $p_t(x)\ge\epsilon\pi(x)$ and
$\pi(x)/p_t(x)\le1/\epsilon$. Exact EVI is myopically optimal only under a
correct posterior, exact update, equal costs, and argmax acquisition. The
implemented finite-temperature candidate-mixture policy has no such
optimality claim, finite-horizon guarantee, or general regret bound. It scores approximate Bayesian risk
reduction, whereas the terminal action uses a confidence set; their alignment
is a required simulation diagnostic, not an identity.

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\begin{proposition}[Sequential unbiasedness]
\label{prop:unbiased}
Assume the finite frame and fixed budget $T$ are prespecified;
\cref{eq:response-stability} holds; outcomes are complete; $p_t$ and
$m_{t-1}$ are measurable before outcome $t$; sampling is with replacement from
the logged $p_t$; and \cref{eq:explore} holds. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\psi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\Delta_F,\qquad
\mathbb E[\widehat\Delta_T]=\Delta_F,
\end{equation}
for any misspecified working jury model or outcome predictor.
\end{proposition}

The proof follows by conditioning on $X_t$ and appears in
\cref{app:datasheet}. Forward or prequential fitting is essential: each
prediction and sampling probability is frozen before its outcome. Ordinary
random $K$-fold fitting after adaptive collection can leak information through
future selections.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-budget inference]
\label{thm:clt}
Under the assumptions of \cref{prop:unbiased}, let
$D_{Tt}=\psi_{Tt}-\Delta_F$. Suppose
\begin{equation}
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb E[D_{Tt}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
\xrightarrow{p}V\in(0,\infty)
\end{equation}
for deterministic $V$, and for every $\eta>0$ the martingale Lindeberg
quantity
\begin{equation}
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb E[D_{Tt}^2\mathbf 1\{|D_{Tt}|>\eta\sqrt T\}
\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
\xrightarrow{p}0.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\sqrt T(\widehat\Delta_T-\Delta_F)
\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal N(0,V).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This is the standard martingale central-limit route
\cite{hall1980martingale}, specialized to predictable adaptive sampling.
Because $Y,m\in[-1,1]$ and $\pi/p\le1/\epsilon$, increments are bounded when
$\epsilon$ is fixed. This boundedness gives the required squared-increment
martingale law of large numbers, so
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_T=\frac1{T-1}\sum_{t=1}^T
(\psi_t-\widehat\Delta_T)^2,\qquad
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}=\sqrt{\widehat V_T/T}
\end{equation}
gives studentized fixed-budget inference. This is pointwise asymptotic, not
exact finite-sample, coverage. The protocol fixes $T$ before the first human
outcome and forbids threshold-based optional stopping.

\subsection{Contamination and Gaming Resistance}

Public static items are checked for exact, normalized, paraphrase, and
template-level overlap with model-accessible corpora where those corpora are
available. Ordering-based tests provide evidence for only particular forms of
memorization \cite{oren2024contamination}; absence of a signal is not proof of
cleanliness. A fresh private wave and generated variants reduce exposure but
create a new population, so longitudinal analyses retain bridge items and
separate within-wave model difference from between-wave drift.

Judge prompts and identities are hidden from evaluated systems. Position is
randomized, response identifiers are stripped, and controlled style/length
transformations probe judge sensitivity. Model outputs containing instructions
to the evaluator are separately flagged. The human-only sealed audit remains
the final check against jury manipulation.

\subsection{Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:statistical-protocol}

When every assignment is complete, the primary two-sided interval uses the
frozen $z_{0.975}=1.959963984540054$:
\begin{equation}
[L_T^{\rm cmp},U_T^{\rm cmp}]=
\left[\widehat\Delta_T\pm
z_{0.975}\sqrt{\widehat V_T/T}\right]\cap[-1,1].
\end{equation}
If any $R_t=0$, define $B_\epsilon=1+2/\epsilon$,
$c_\alpha=B_\epsilon\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)/T}$, and use instead
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_T^{\rm miss}
&=\max\left\{-1,\frac1T\sum_t\psi_t^- -c_\alpha\right\},\\
U_T^{\rm miss}
&=\min\left\{1,\frac1T\sum_t\psi_t^+ +c_\alpha\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:missing-interval}
\end{equation}
Each endpoint contribution lies in $[-B_\epsilon,B_\epsilon]$ and its
predictable mean bounds $\Delta_F$; two one-sided martingale--Hoeffding bounds
give coverage at least $1-\alpha$ without a response-mechanism assumption.
Write $[L_T,U_T]$ for the applicable interval. It is widened by $\gamma$ in
the response-drift sensitivity analysis.
This guarantee is intentionally severe: at the pilot values
$\epsilon=0.2$, $T=1000$, and $\alpha=0.05$, $c_\alpha=0.9448$.
Consequently even one missing outcome will generally force
$\mathrm{NE}$; simulation must report this degeneracy rather than presenting
the bound as label-efficient.
The terminal action used with \cref{eq:loss} and in the final report is
\begin{equation}
\widehat a=
\begin{cases}
A,&L_T>\delta,\\
B,&U_T<-\delta,\\
E,&[L_T,U_T]\subseteq[-\delta,\delta],\\
\mathrm{NE},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For complete outcomes this wrong-action probability is at most
$\alpha+o(1)$; with \cref{eq:missing-interval} it is at most $\alpha$ under
response stability, for this single comparison.
This does not bound
abstention, expected stakeholder loss, or multi-model ranking error.

\Cref{thm:clt} is conditional on the full realized frame, including the enrolled
rater panel and fixed response pairs. For a separate deployment-population
target $\Delta_{\mathrm{pop}}$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat\Delta_T-\Delta_{\mathrm{pop}}
=(\widehat\Delta_T-\Delta_F)+(\Delta_F-\Delta_{\mathrm{pop}}).
\end{equation}
The martingale variance covers only the first term. The second requires the
actual first-stage item and rater recruitment design and its replicate weights
or a justified superpopulation model; prompt variants cannot be bootstrapped
as independent root items.

\section{Empirical Study}
\label{sec:results}

Simulation precedes human collection. We vary $\Delta_F$ at
$\{-\delta,0,\delta\}$ and local alternatives around both decision boundaries.
A full Cartesian crossing of all stressors is neither computationally credible
nor the registered design. Instead, a frozen 16-run two-level fraction varies
tie prevalence, item and judge-family correlation, repeated-rater variation,
nonresponse, rare-stratum mass, exploration, and temporal drift. Its eight
main-effect columns are balanced and orthogonal, with no main effect aliased
with a two-factor interaction; two-factor interactions remain aliased. We
cross those rows with three boundary effects and three working-model regimes,
then add center-condition boundary and local-alternative blocks, for 189
conditions total.

For each condition, 2,000 Monte Carlo trials compare target-random human
sampling, static independent calibration, Active Testing, control-variate
evaluation, PPAT-style residual acquisition, variance-optimal residual
sampling, pure uncertainty sampling, and EVI. Within a condition and trial,
policies share the latent frame and complete potential-outcome stream but use
policy-keyed randomization. Each acquisition policy is crossed with the same
valid \cref{eq:estimator}; augmentation is then ablated at fixed policy. Exact
DGP and policy implementations remain hash-blocked. Until faithful native
implementations are frozen, Active Testing, control-variate, and PPAT names
refer only to score-inspired proposal adaptations, not reproductions of those
methods. Thus this frozen layout is
not evidence that simulations ran. Primary
metrics are bias, RMSE, 95\% coverage, interval width, false decisive actions,
equivalence decisions, abstention, realized loss, maximum weight, and ESS,
with Monte Carlo uncertainty. The simulation separately reports whether
Bayes-risk acquisition actually lowers realized loss under the terminal
confidence-set action.

The planned public study freezes all 200 roots at the pinned LiveBench
reasoning revision, 200 hash-ranked roots from math, and all 128 coding roots:
a 528-root weighted public-benchmark frame. Each source receives mass $1/3$
and roots are uniform within source. This is neither a product-domain sample
nor a deployment population. It uses two validated prompt forms per item and
paired response draws from two fixed open model revisions. Four model-judge families
score all cells in both orders. The roots induce 2,112
root--prompt--order cells, 8,448 jury calls, and 168,960 human target units
after crossing the 80-rater panel; human labels sample this frame rather than
fully crossing it. A 200-rating target-random human pilot precedes 800
sequential ratings; a separate 200-rating target-random audit sampled from
\cref{eq:target-mass} remains sealed. All counts and fixed budget $T=1000$
assignment attempts are set by simulation before the first human outcome; a
missing response is not replaced. A frozen panel of 80 raters
is recruited against a written definition, assigned
target weights, trained on a fixed handbook, and screened with non-study
examples. The expected load is 12.5 primary attempts per rater; realized load,
withdrawals, and response are reported. All $T$ assignment slots are
preassigned. Rater unavailability, withdrawal, or a binding quota records
$R_t=0$ in its original slot; no replacement occurs, later slots continue, and
the analysis remains at fixed $T$ using the predeclared partial-identification
bounds.
Identifiers are hidden, both display orders are balanced in expectation, and
every skip or nonresponse is retained. Attempt position is randomized, sealed
audit attempts are interleaved at hidden prespecified slots, and the drift
sensitivity grid is frozen before labels.

\subsection{Robustness and Ablations}

We ablate augmentation, EVI, the exploration floor, tie modeling, root-item
effects, judge-family effects, and prequential fitting while crossing each
acquisition policy with a common estimator. An exploratory extension would
train acquisition on older model pairs and test a preregistered held-out
stronger pair to expose frontier
shift. Additional falsification tests swap AB/BA order, control response length
and style, hold out prompt families, remove one judge family, change the
practical margin, and repeat the study on a fresh temporal wave.

Model checks use held-out log score, multiclass Brier score, tie calibration,
swap symmetry, posterior predictive checks, and residuals by domain, order,
length, family, judge, and item. Disagreement cases are sampled by a
prespecified stratified rule rather than selected for narrative convenience.
SCOPE-style bidirectional conformal abstention is included as a jury-only
diagnostic and never substitutes for the human estimand.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The finite frame may omit important deployment requests, and a convenience
rater panel supports inference only to that panel. Human preference is not
factual correctness, safety, fairness, or welfare. The Davidson model can be
misspecified, judge correlations can be more complex than modeled, and
non-transitivity can make broader rankings incoherent. Sequential unbiasedness does
not guarantee useful finite-sample intervals when exploration probabilities
are small.

The method requires exact logging of every adaptive probability and a fixed
budget or sequentially valid interval. EVI may save no labels and can be worse
than target-random sampling under model shift. A sealed audit consumes additional
human effort. Fresh items reduce exposure but impair longitudinal
comparability, while black-box contamination cannot be ruled out. Finally, one
pairwise replacement decision does not establish a general benchmark or model
ordering.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We framed language-model comparison as a decision about a frozen, weighted
human panel rather than a latent jury score. Randomized exploration and
draw-level sequential augmentation permit outcome-adaptive human acquisition
without surrendering design identification under explicit assumptions. The
formal result does not show that acquisition is efficient or that the working
model is valid. The decisive next evidence is coverage under misspecified,
correlated simulations followed by prospective concordance with a sealed target-random human
audit.

\section*{Impact Statement}

Evaluation methods shape which systems are deployed and which capabilities are
optimized. A narrow or unrepresentative item and rater frame can legitimize a
harmful deployment even when its confidence interval is statistically valid.
Adaptive labeling may undersample rare communities unless target weights and
the exploration floor preserve them. Raters may encounter disturbing content;
they require informed consent, task-specific warnings, the ability to skip
without penalty, fair compensation, and access to support. Deployment logs
must be de-identified and retained under strict access controls. Public
reporting will include the population definition, tie rate, abstentions,
maximum sampling weights, subgroup support, and unresolved validity threats.
The method is not intended to turn human preference into a universal quality
score or to justify deployment without separate safety and factuality audits.

\subsection{Proof of \cref{prop:unbiased}}
Conditioning on pre-outcome history,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E[\psi_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
&=\sum_xp_t(x)\left[
\bar m_{t-1}+\frac{\pi(x)}{p_t(x)}
\{\mu_x-m_{t-1}(x)\}\right]\\
&=\bar m_{t-1}+\sum_x\pi(x)\{\mu_x-m_{t-1}(x)\}
=\Delta_F.
\end{align}
Averaging over fixed $T$ proves the second statement. Neither step requires a
correct jury model.

\subsection{Consistency of the Variance Estimate}
Let $D_{Tt}=\psi_{Tt}-\Delta_F$. Fixed $\epsilon>0$ makes $D_{Tt}$ uniformly
bounded. Hence
$D_{Tt}^2-\mathbb E[D_{Tt}^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]$ is a bounded martingale
difference and its average converges to zero in probability. The conditional
variance assumption in \cref{thm:clt} then yields
$T^{-1}\sum_tD_{Tt}^2\to_p V$; replacing $\Delta_F$ by
$\widehat\Delta_T$ changes the centered second moment by $o_p(1)$. Therefore
$\widehat V_T\to_pV$ and Slutsky's theorem justifies the stated studentized
interval.

\subsection{Human Audit}
The audit draws complete $(i,c,h,o)$ units from $\pi$ before adaptation and is
stored behind a separate random seed. It estimates the same finite-frame target
from 200 fixed audit slots by the unweighted sample mean. Missing audit outcomes
are replaced by $-1$ and $+1$ for lower and upper endpoint means, with
one-sided Hoeffding radius
$\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)/200}$; they are never dropped or replaced. If
operational assignment changes the draw law, the exact target-to-draw ratio is
retained and the corresponding bounded-weight interval is used.
Audit membership never removes a unit from adaptive support: only the audit
seed, assignments, and outcomes are hidden. Assignment seeds are independent,
but persistent unobserved shared-rater session effects would violate
\cref{eq:response-stability} unless included in the predictable history or
conditioned upon. A clustered shared-rater contrast is therefore an
assumption-violating sensitivity analysis, not a repair of the primary
martingale claim.
Its outcomes are opened only after the adaptive algorithm, model
hyperparameters, and primary analysis are frozen. An adaptive-minus-audit
equivalence test remains blocked until its margin, dependence model, and
failure rule are frozen.
